✅ Primary Finding
Service learning programmes significantly improve student SDG awareness (Mann-Whitney U = 8,241, p = 0.003). Effect size r = 0.42 indicates medium-to-large practical significance.
📌 Satisfaction Driver
Students in health-sector NGOs reported 23% higher satisfaction than non-health NGOs (Mdn = 4.4 vs 3.8, p < 0.001). NGO mentorship quality is the strongest predictor.
📈 5-Year Trend
Programme completion rate improved from 71.2% (2021) to 89.4% (2025). Satisfaction scores show consistent upward trend (Kruskal-Wallis H = 42.8, p < 0.001).

Radar
🏅 Top Faculty
Medicine & Nursing students report the highest satisfaction (Mdn = 4.5), closely followed by Public Health (Mdn = 4.4). Both show strong SDG 3 alignment.
📊 Faculty Gap
Engineering faculty students show lowest satisfaction (Mdn = 3.6) but highest career reconsideration rates (71.2%), suggesting these placements are transformative despite lower immediate satisfaction.
🎯 Recommendation
Faculty-specific NGO matching algorithms could improve alignment. Engineering students paired with health-tech NGOs show 18% higher satisfaction than general health placements.
🗃️ Dataset Overview
  • 9,192 student-NGO placement records (2021–2025)
  • 47 NGO partners across 8 Indian cities
  • Data from SCEW institutional programme database
  • Anonymised per institutional ethics approval
  • Sourced: HealthConfluence 2026 study protocol
🔧 Mixed-Methods Design
  • QUAN: Pre-post Likert surveys (1–5 scale)
  • QUAL: Open-ended reflection journals (IPA)
  • SPSS v28 for all inferential statistics
  • Thematic analysis — Braun & Clarke (2006) framework
  • Triangulation of quantitative and qualitative findings
📐 Statistical Approach
  • Normality tested: Shapiro-Wilk (W = 0.94, p < 0.01)
  • Non-parametric tests selected for ordinal data
  • Mann-Whitney U: two-group comparisons
  • Kruskal-Wallis H: multi-group comparisons (k > 2)
  • Post-hoc: Dunn's test + Bonferroni correction
  • Effect size: r = Z/√N (Fritz et al., 2012)
🔍 Thematic Analysis
  • Interpretative Phenomenological Analysis (IPA)
  • n = 342 reflection journal entries coded
  • Two independent coders (κ = 0.84)
  • 3 superordinate themes emerged iteratively
  • Member checking conducted with 28 participants
🌍 SDG Framework
  • UN 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development
  • SDG mapping instrument: validated 20-item scale
  • Primary focus: SDGs 3, 4, 10, 17
  • Student-NGO activity logs cross-coded to SDG targets
  • Alignment threshold: ≥ 40% activity overlap
⚠️ Limitations
  • Single-institution student sample limits generalisability
  • Social desirability bias in self-report surveys
  • No control group (ethical constraints)
  • SDG self-assessment subject to construct bias
  • Qualitative sample (n=342) may under-represent views
